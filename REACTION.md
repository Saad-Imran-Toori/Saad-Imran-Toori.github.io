# The one real person

**Who:** Mansoor — a teammate from the DecisAI build (CUST Hackathon 2026).
**Why him:** he can judge two things at once — whether the claim lands on someone in my field,
and whether the DecisAI case reads true to someone who was actually there.
**What I asked:** the live link, and three deliberately vague questions. I didn't tell him what to
look for, because then I'd only have learned whether he could follow instructions.

---

## What he said, in his words

> **What do I think you actually do?**
>
> You build AI systems that work with company documents, especially retrieval/RAG systems where
> the AI only answers when there's actual evidence in the source material. The main thing I took
> away is that you care about making AI reliable rather than just making it answer everything.

> **What was the first thing I noticed?**
>
> The line "AI that answers only from your documents" immediately stood out. It tells me your niche
> very quickly. I also noticed that the portfolio feels more like a focused specialist's site than a
> typical software-engineering-student portfolio with a huge list of technologies.

> **Anything confusing or boring?**
>
> The positioning is clear, but it may actually be too narrow. After reading it, I'd assume you
> mainly want to work on document retrieval/RAG systems. If you can also do broader ML, AI
> engineering, backend work, etc., I wouldn't really know that from the site. Visually/content-wise,
> the project descriptions are thoughtful, but there aren't many projects, so after the two case
> studies I felt like I'd already seen most of what the portfolio had to offer.

---

## What I take from it

**The claim landed.** He described my work back to me without using my words, and he reached the
part I actually care about on his own: *"you care about making AI reliable rather than just making
it answer everything."* That is the refusal behaviour, understood from the site alone. This was the
one thing I most wanted to test — item 17 on my "still ugly" list — and it passed.

**The positioning read as intended.** "More like a focused specialist's site than a typical
software-engineering-student portfolio with a huge list of technologies" is close to a restatement
of the decision I made in Week 1, when the pressure-test told me to lead with the refusal rather
than with a technology list.

**"Too narrow" is the finding, and it is not simply a bug to fix.** Mansoor is reading the site the
way a recruiter would, where breadth reduces risk. I built it for the buyer I named in Week 1 — a
founder or operations lead at a document-heavy company — and for that reader, narrow is the whole
point. Both readings are legitimate; they are different audiences. What his comment actually proves
is that the site currently serves one of them and is silent about the other, and that I had not
consciously decided that. I have now: the site stays aimed at the document-heavy buyer, because a
site that speaks to everyone earns no email from anyone. But I am recording that this is a choice
with a cost, not an oversight.

**"There aren't many projects" is a fair hit with no defence.** Two cases is what I have. A third
arrives in Week 8 when the FlyRank capstone ships. Until then, thin is accurate.

## What I changed because of it

One sentence, in the About block on the home page. Re-reading his answer, the site *already* shows
breadth — the FlyRank case is a ranking and validation problem, not a retrieval one at all — but
every frame on the page funnels toward the document claim, so he never registered it as such. So the
fix was not to add breadth; it was to stop hiding the breadth already on the page:

> "I work on the retrieval layer — the part that decides whether there is enough evidence to answer
> at all. That's where I go deepest, but not all of it: the second case below is a ranking and
> validation problem, not a retrieval one. Same standard applies to both."

**What I did not change:** I did not add a technology list to prove breadth. That is precisely the
"huge list of technologies" he identified as the thing my site is better than — I would have fixed
his second comment by breaking the first one.
