Case 01 · CUST Hackathon 2026 · team of three · my part: the retrieval layer

DecisAI — a bid assistant that refuses to answer without evidence

Proposal teams get tenders running to hundreds of pages. Someone reads every line, pulls out the requirements, and decides whether the company can actually deliver — against a deadline.

A company uploads its own bid history and capability records first. I chunked and embedded those with Sentence Transformers into ChromaDB, so each extracted requirement can be matched against what the company has genuinely done before. Claude Haiku runs it online; Ollama handles the offline case, so a tender never has to leave the building.

A validator sits at the end of the pipeline and rejects invented capability IDs, unsupported claims and fabricated metrics. When it fires, the requirement is marked No match found · EVIDENCE GAP and shown to the user — including the AI's own confidence, at 35%, rather than hiding it.

What I'd fix first: past about 1,500 pages, retrieval is still slow. That is the honest limit and it is the next thing I would work on.

Case 02 · FlyRank AI · machine learning internship · July – August 2026

Which of 16,726 pages should an editor open first?

An editor can realistically review about fifty pages a week. That is a 335-week backlog, so the useful question is not "is this page underperforming" — thousands are. It is which one to open first.

I trained a gradient-boosted ranker on three months of Google Search Console data, 40,152 pages across 24 clients, validated with five-fold client-grouped cross-validation so no client appeared on both sides of a split. It beats the frozen rule in every fold.

And the caveats sit in the same paragraph as the result: the fold spread runs 0.60 to 0.90, roughly half the signal is click history, about half the portfolio is flagged watch-only, and nothing here is causal. The ranking is a reviewer aid, never an automatic publishing decision.

Case 03 · Software Construction & Design · Java desktop

AssetForge Studio — project tracking built around the thing Jira ignores

Indie game teams track tasks in one tool and assets in a folder somewhere. AssetForge puts both in one JavaFX desktop app: seven modules covering dashboard, projects, phases, a Kanban board, an asset library with duplicate and missing-file detection, a bug tracker and a report generator.

An eight-entity relational schema behind MVC, DAO and service layers, with Observer driving live dashboard updates, Factory for reports and Strategy for export formats. Tested with JUnit 5 against in-memory H2.

The part I care about is the redesign: four swappable palettes — light, dark, midnight and a high-contrast theme for low-vision users — all driven by lookup tokens, with every change confined to the view layer. The backend has no reference to JavaFX anywhere.
